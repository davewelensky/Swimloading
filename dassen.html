Dassen Island is Africa's largest seabird island and home to 60,000 penguins. The boat takes you out to the island — you touch the rocks to start, then swim 8.7km back to Yzerfontein on the mainland. Cold Atlantic water, open ocean swell, a dramatic wildlife start.

Route Description

01
Start — Dassen Island
Boat out to Dassen Island — Africa's largest seabird sanctuary, home to 60,000 penguins. Touch the rocks at the island to start. Do NOT land — Dassen is strictly protected (SANCCOB-managed) and landing without permit is prohibited. Swimmers must remain in the water at the rocks. Cold entry — Benguela upwelling keeps temps at 13–16°C year-round.
02
The crossing — open Atlantic
8.7km open ocean crossing heading east toward the mainland. The NW swell is generally behind you on this direction, which can help. The channel is fully exposed to the Atlantic fetch — conditions can build quickly once the sea breeze fills in. Stay close to your support vessel throughout. Navigation: the Yzerfontein coastline and lighthouse are visible from Dassen Island in clear conditions.
03
Arrival — Yzerfontein
Finish at Yzerfontein — touch the beach or slipway as agreed with your support vessel skipper before the swim. The mainland coastline becomes visible from well out in the channel — aim for the harbour and slipway area. Strong southerly chop in the final 2km is common if the afternoon wind has filled in.
04
Boat briefing — outbound then follow
The support vessel departs Yzerfontein with the swimmer aboard, transfers to Dassen Island for the start, then follows the swimmer back to Yzerfontein. Confirm finish point with the skipper before departure — it's easy to drift north or south of the slipway in the final kilometre. A secondary kayak escort alongside is strongly recommended.

Key Requirements

01
Swell below 1.5m — critical threshold
The West Coast Atlantic is exposed to swell from NW storm systems in the South Atlantic. Swell above 1.5m at Yzerfontein makes the crossing uncomfortable and navigation difficult. Above 2.5m is a no-go. Swell tends to be lower in summer (Oct–Mar) when high pressure systems dominate.
02
Wind below 25 km/h
NW wind (the typical prevailing direction here) can assist the outbound crossing but creates heavy return conditions for the boat and makes the rock approach at Dassen dangerous. SE wind (Cape Doctor) blows offshore from the swimmer's perspective on the outbound leg — it can create confused seas in the channel. Light conditions (under 15 km/h) are ideal.
03
Cold water tolerance — 13–16°C for 2–3.5 hours
The Benguela upwelling makes the West Coast significantly colder than False Bay. Temps rarely exceed 17°C here — summer average is 14–15°C. This is a serious cold water swim. Acclimatise thoroughly before attempt. Cold shock entry protocol essential. Below 13°C, hypothermia risk for a 2.5h+ skins swim is significant.
04
SANCCOB / permit for island approach
Dassen Island is managed as a seabird sanctuary. Contact SANCCOB (Southern African Foundation for the Conservation of Coastal Birds) and the relevant maritime authority before planning your swim. Vessel approach to the island must be cleared. Do not disturb the penguin colony.
05
Support vessel with Atlantic ocean experience
Open ocean, 7km from shore, exposed Atlantic — a motorised support vessel is non-negotiable. Your skipper must be comfortable in West Coast conditions and understand Benguela current patterns. A kayak escort in addition to the motorised vessel is strongly recommended. File a float plan before departure.

Wildlife — 60,000 Penguins and More

Dassen Island hosts the largest African penguin colony on earth — approximately 60,000 birds. Reaching the island rocks you will be surrounded by penguins at the waterline. This is one of the most extraordinary wildlife encounters in open water swimming anywhere in the world.

Also expect:
· Cape fur seals — colony on the island, curious in the water
· Cormorants, gannets, terns — the island has massive seabird colonies
· Great white sharks — present in the West Coast channel. Risk must be assessed and safety protocols agreed with your team. The seal colony on Dassen is a known food source for sharks in the area.
· Dolphins — common escort companions in the channel
· Sunfish (Mola mola) — occasionally seen in West Coast waters

Cold Water Adjustment — Applied Automatically

Cold water (below 16°C) creates a physiological cost even for acclimatised swimmers. Expect your open water pace to be slower than in warmer conditions:

· 16°C: ~1 min/km slower than 20°C pace
· 15°C: ~2 min/km slower
· 14°C: ~2–3 min/km slower
· 13°C: ~3–4 min/km slower (significant)

The prediction uses your stated pace as-is. If your pace is from a warmer swim, manually add the cold water adjustment above.
