What We Know
01
18km · West Coast Atlantic
The West Coast Angle is an 18km swim in cold Benguela Atlantic water on the West Coast of South Africa. All 4 recorded completions were done at 13–14°C. This is cold by any standard — below 14°C for the duration. The name refers to a specific geographic angle (headland or coastal bend) along the West Coast.
02
Best seasons: March and autumn
The 4 completions span March 2025 (Good), February 2026 (Misty), and April 2026 (Good). Conditions were manageable in all three events. February is colder (13°C) and mist is possible — the Benguela upwelling brings cold, nutrient-rich water and associated coastal fog. March and April have slightly warmer water (14°C) and typically better visibility.
03
Very cold — serious preparation required
13–14°C for 6–7 hours is an extreme cold exposure. Even experienced skins swimmers require significant cold acclimatisation before attempting this. A wetsuit adds only modest speed benefit (Kay-Lee Mouton in wetsuit was slightly slower than Schalk du Plessis in skins — conditions were worse on her attempt). Medical and safety protocol must be agreed in advance.

Key Requirements
01
Swell below 1.5m — go threshold
West Coast Atlantic is exposed to NW swell from South Atlantic storms. All completions occurred in Good or Misty conditions — no rough swell events in the record. Keep swell below 1.5m for a safe attempt. Above 2.5m is a no-go.
02
Wind below 25 km/h — early start essential
West Coast winds build through the day. The SE sea breeze and NW afternoon winds can both create difficult conditions by midday. Start between 05:00 and 07:00 to complete the first 12km before conditions deteriorate. A 6–7 hour swim in rising wind is significantly harder than the same swim in flat morning conditions.
03
Cold water tolerance — 13–14°C for 6–7 hours
The most demanding aspect of this swim. Even in wetsuits, 13°C for 7+ hours (Julian Honeywill, 7:18) is near the limit of safe exposure. Skins swimmers need exceptional cold tolerance. Hypothermia risk management: agree on abort criteria with support vessel skipper before starting. Core temp monitoring recommended.
04
Experienced support vessel — 18km offshore
Open Atlantic, no shelter, 18km from start. The support skipper must have West Coast open ocean experience and understand Benguela conditions. Navigation in coastal fog (Misty conditions occur) requires GPS and VHF radio. A secondary kayak escort is strongly recommended.

Cold Water Pace Adjustment
At 13–14°C, physiological cooling begins even in experienced swimmers within 2–3 hours. Expect pace to slow in the second half of the swim:

· 14°C: ~2–3 min/km slower than 20°C pace
· 13°C: ~3–5 min/km slower (significant over 18km)
· Skins vs wetsuit: wetsuit insulates better but adds little speed at cold temp paces

The prediction uses your stated pace as-is. If your benchmark is from warmer water, add 2–4 min/km for cold water adjustment.
