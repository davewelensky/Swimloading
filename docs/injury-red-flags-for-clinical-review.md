# Injury red flags: for clinical review

Please review before this feature goes live on SwimLoading. It is shown to adult amateur open-water swimmers preparing for long crossings, mostly in South Africa.

**What it does.** When a swimmer records an injury or soreness, they first see the list below. If they tick anything, they are shown the action for that tier and no training guidance is given. AI-generated training guidance is only offered if nothing is ticked. The app never names a condition or suggests a cause.

**Where it came from.** Assembled from UK primary-care and NHS patient sources written for general patients. No athlete-specific list was found.

**What we need from you.** Add, remove or reword anything, and confirm the tier for each item. The specific questions are at the end.

## Call emergency services now

Shown to the swimmer: Call your local emergency number. Do not drive yourself.

- [ ] Chest pain that is tight or squeezing, or spreading to your arms, neck or jaw
- [ ] Severe difficulty breathing
- [ ] Pale, blue or grey skin or lips
- [ ] You have collapsed

## Get emergency assessment today

Shown to the swimmer: Go to an emergency department or see a doctor today. Do not swim until you have been assessed.

- [ ] After an injury, you think a bone may be damaged
- [ ] After an injury, a shoulder looks out of place and has not gone back
- [ ] Red skin over the joint with fever or feeling generally unwell
- [ ] Significant weakness in one or both arms, with neck or arm pain
- [ ] Nerve pain increasing in both arms together
- [ ] Unsteady on your feet, with neck or arm pain

## Get medical advice as soon as possible

Shown to the swimmer: Speak to a doctor as soon as you can. Hold off on training until you have.

- [ ] Your hands are getting clumsier
- [ ] Worsening numbness, pins and needles or weakness in one or both arms
- [ ] Balance or walking problems since neck pain began
- [ ] Sudden weakness in an arm or shoulder
- [ ] Suddenly unable to raise your arm after an injury
- [ ] A lump, swelling or unexplained change in shape
- [ ] Pain at night
- [ ] New shoulder pain and a serious past illness a doctor should know about
- [ ] Pain getting worse even though you have cut back training
- [ ] Aching and stiffness in several areas beyond the shoulder

## See a physiotherapist or doctor

Shown to the swimmer: Book a physiotherapist or doctor before building your training any further.

- [ ] Your shoulder partly or completely slips out
- [ ] Neck pain or stiffness that has not gone away after a few weeks
- [ ] No improvement after 6 to 12 weeks of exercise

## In-water note

Shown beside the list: In the water: stop and alert your escort or crew for any chest symptoms, new arm weakness or numbness, or unexpected breathlessness. Breathlessness with a cough or pink, frothy or bloody sputum means leaving the water.

## Soreness bands (unvalidated)

The swimmer picks one when no red flag applies. No study has tested these on swimmers. The bands were assembled from a pain-monitoring approach and a yardage progression written for other groups.

| Band | Reported soreness | Training response |
|---|---|---|
| green | No pain, or a mild ache gone by the end of warm-up | Continue, and keep increases gradual. |
| amber | Pain lasts into warm-up, for over an hour after swimming, or into the next day | Repeat rather than progress. Cut distance, remove paddles and vary your strokes. |
| red_amber | Getting worse week on week | Stop that session and reduce. Arrange a physiotherapist review. |
| refer | No improvement over several weeks | Arrange a physiotherapist or doctor review before building further. |

## Questions for you

1. Should sudden one-sided arm weakness or numbness sit in the emergency tier? We could not confirm this from a source we read, and need wording from a national health-service source.
2. How should "a serious past illness a doctor should know about" be worded? The source names one specific illness.
3. Numbness or weakness after long cold swims is easy to dismiss as cold or fatigue. Should it go to the urgent tier if it persists or worsens, and after how long?
4. Should the soreness bands include a pain score? The source figure is 3 to 5 out of 10, borrowed from research on another tendon. It is currently left out.
5. What should the emergency and referral wording be for South Africa? The list currently says "your local emergency number".
