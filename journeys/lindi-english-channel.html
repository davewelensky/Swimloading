Lindi grew up swimming in South African ocean waters — Cape Town bays, Langebaan lagoon, coastlines where the cold Atlantic meets the warmer Indian Ocean. Now she is training in the lidos and open seas of the UK, building toward one of swimming's great challenges: a solo unassisted crossing of the English Channel.

Every session is logged on SwimLoading. Every cold water breakthrough, every open sea condition, every temperature reading — building the record that crossing day will be made from.

Training milestones
April 2026 · Langebaan, South Africa
Building the base in warm water
Before arriving in the UK, Lindi banked volume in Langebaan lagoon — warm, flat water where you can swim long and think about nothing except technique and breathing. The Channel requires endurance above everything else. Cold tolerance comes after the base is solid.
Endurance base
2 May 2026 · Heron Lake, UK
UK season opens — the acclimatisation begins
First UK open water session of the season at Heron Lake, 15.5°C. Air temperature 11.5°C. "Cool air but warmed up nicely" — the transition from South African warmth to UK cold has a beginning. The body starts learning. Each session after this is a step deeper into what the Channel will feel like.
UK season start
16 May 2026 · Tooting Bec Lido, London
13.5°C — the cold breakthrough
Cold air, windy, 13.5°C water. The English Channel averages 15–18°C during the crossing window. At 13.5°C, Lindi has already proven she can swim in temperatures colder than anything the Channel is likely to throw at her. This is the session that changed the question from "can she handle the cold?" to "how much stronger does she get?"
13.5°C — coldest logged
3–4 June 2026 · Studland & Knoll Beach, Dorset
Open sea — between the gales
The Channel is not a lido. It has chop, swell, current, ships and weather that doesn't wait. Lindi's Dorset sessions were the first real Channel simulation — "finding calm water inbetween gale force!" at Knoll Beach. South Beach Studland at 15°C, then Knoll Beach at 16°C, reading conditions and adapting. Exactly what crossing day will demand.
Open sea conditions
June 2026 · Tooting Bec Lido — ongoing
Building the routine
Multiple sessions per week at Tooting Bec, temperatures rising toward 19°C as summer arrives. Two sessions on June 15 — Tooting Bec then Heron Lake. This is the consistency phase: showing up, accumulating hours, keeping the body tuned. The Channel is a long way from London. But it starts here.

16 May · Tooting Bec Lido
What's coming next
The 6-hour qualifying swim
A solo Channel attempt requires a witnessed 6-hour continuous swim in water at or below 16°C, observed by a Channel Swimming Association official. Dover Harbour and Tooting Bec are both approved venues. This is the next major target in the preparation — and the window is approaching.
Dover Harbour training
The CSA runs Saturday morning sessions from Dover — the actual water of the crossing. Tides, Channel temperature, community of other aspirants training in the same stretch of sea. A session at Dover is different from anywhere else in the world.
Building toward 4-hour sessions
A Channel crossing takes between 9 and 25 hours depending on conditions. The body's ability to keep moving after hour 4, 8, 12 is trained through long, deliberate sessions. The next phase of Lindi's preparation extends duration — 2 hours, then 3, building toward the 4-hour swims that make crossing times credible.
