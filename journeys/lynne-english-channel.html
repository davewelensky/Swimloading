Lake Geneva season opens
The season begins at Promenthoux on Lake Geneva — Lynne's home training base. Lake Geneva is a serious preparation environment: long, deep, cold-prone water with real weather. Not a lido, not a bay. It demands respect. This is where the Channel prep is built, session by session.

9°C — well below Channel temperature
The English Channel averages 15–18°C during the crossing window. At Gland Plage, Lynne logged 9°C — six degrees colder than anything the Channel is likely to throw at her on crossing day. Cold tolerance isn't a question. The question is execution.

4–5 June 2026 · Boscombe Beach & Studland, Dorset
UK sea training — Channel water
Lake Geneva builds fitness and cold tolerance. The Channel requires something else: open sea movement, tidal current, salt water buoyancy, and the psychological weight of no visible far shore. Lynne's Dorset sessions — Boscombe Beach and South Beach Studland at 14–16°C — were Channel-specific. The same water, the same coastline, the same conditions she will cross in August.

What's coming next
Window opens 19 August 2026
The crossing window is a slot assigned by the Channel Swimming Association — tides, weather, pilot boat availability all align. Lynne's opens 19 August. Everything from here is about arriving at that date ready.
The 6-hour qualifying swim
A CSA-observed solo 6-hour continuous swim in water at or below 16°C is required before the crossing attempt. Lake Geneva at 9°C means the cold threshold is long behind her. The duration is the target now.
Crossing day
34km of English Channel. Dover to Calais. An average crossing takes 12–16 hours. Lynne has already proved she can handle the cold, the distance, and the dedication. What remains is the tide and the weather and the swim itself.
