Key Requirements — Double Crossing
01
Water temp 13°C+ (skins)
The Atlantic at Big Bay is Benguela upwelling — cold, year-round. Typical range 12–16°C. March is usually 14–16°C but can spike 13°C with SE upwelling. Cold shock on entry is a real risk — acclimatise gradually. Hypothermia risk increases sharply below 13°C for a 3.5+ hour swim.
02
Tidal timing — this is everything
The channel between Big Bay and Robben Island has strong tidal streams. Slack water or favourable flow makes a 30-minute difference per leg. Start the outbound leg on a slack or flood (rising) tide. The return leg needs to align with the next tidal window — plan ~3.5–7 hours total for a double. Check the tides tab.
03
Wind — light is best
The Cape Doctor (SE) is the dominant wind at Big Bay — 25–40km/h SE creates significant chop and can push swimmers off line. N/NW winds are less common but more favourable for navigation. Any wind above 30km/h makes a double extremely difficult. Check the 7-day window.
04
Swell below 1.5m
Atlantic swell from the SW adds significant difficulty and navigation challenge. 1.5m or below is comfortable. 2m is challenging but manageable for experienced swimmers. Above 2.5m is a no-go for a double.
05
Support boat & safety
A support vessel is essential for navigation (Robben Island approach has restricted zones) and safety. The boat must be cleared with SANCCOB and the Robben Island ferry authorities. Swimmer must not land on the island.
