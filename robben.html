Key Requirements — Double Crossing
01
Water temp 13°C+ (skins)
The Atlantic at Big Bay is Benguela upwelling — cold, year-round. Typical range 12–16°C. March is usually 14–16°C but can spike 13°C with SE upwelling. Cold shock on entry is a real risk — acclimatise gradually. Hypothermia risk increases sharply below 13°C for a 3.5+ hour swim.
02
Tidal timing — this is everything
The channel between Big Bay and Robben Island has strong tidal streams. Slack water or favourable flow makes a 30-minute difference per leg. Start the outbound leg on a slack or flood (rising) tide. The return leg needs to align with the next tidal window — plan ~3.5–7 hours total for a double. Check the tides tab.
03
Wind — light is best
The Cape Doctor (SE) is the dominant wind at Big Bay — 25–40km/h SE creates significant chop and can push swimmers off line. N/NW winds are less common but more favourable for navigation. Any wind above 30km/h makes a double extremely difficult. Check the 7-day window.
04
Swell below 1.5m
Atlantic swell from the SW adds significant difficulty and navigation challenge. 1.5m or below is comfortable. 2m is challenging but manageable for experienced swimmers. Above 2.5m is a no-go for a double.
05
Support boat & safety
A support vessel is essential for navigation (Robben Island approach has restricted zones) and safety. The boat must be cleared with SANCCOB and the Robben Island ferry authorities. Swimmer must not land on the island.

Route: Big Bay (Bloubergstrand) → Murray's Bay Harbour (east side, Robben Island) → Big Bay. Touch the rocks at the harbour wall — physically reaching land is required for the crossing to be valid.

Navigation note: On the outbound leg, use Table Mountain as a bearing reference behind you. On the return, aim for the Blouberg/Big Bay shoreline — your escort skipper holds the line. Current can push you significantly north or south; correct continuously and do not fight it alone.
Double Crossing — Rules & Validation
What counts as a valid double
Start: From land (clear water entry, own power)
Outbound: Mainland → Robben Island — must physically reach land (exit water or clearly touch rocks/beach under observation)
Turn: Regroup briefly, quick feed, back in — the swim must remain continuous in intent and execution
Return: Robben Island → same mainland side
Finish: Exit water unassisted on mainland
The 10-minute rule — what it actually means
This is not a rest allowance — it's a control mechanism to prevent abuse of breaks. The underlying principle CLDSA enforces is simple: the swim must be continuous and unassisted. The ~10 minutes is just a proxy for that.
✓Brief regroup at the island — allowed
✓Quick feed at the turn — allowed
✓Hanging on a bottle or rope for seconds to minutes during feeds — allowed
✗Sitting around recovering — not allowed
✗Warming up excessively — not allowed
✗Resetting like it's a new swim — not allowed
✗Out of water or stationary for more than ~10 minutes — becomes two swims, not a double
Equipment (skins category)
✓Standard costume only — no wetsuit, no thermal suits, no neoprene
✓One swim cap (layering for warmth allowed, not for buoyancy)
✓Goggles allowed
✗No fins, paddles, or propulsion aids
✗No touching boat, crew, or support equipment
Feeding
Feeds from escort boat only — bottle on rope or pole. No physical contact with crew during feeds.
Support requirements
Primary escort boat is non-negotiable — not optional safety theatre. Skipper must know Atlantic currents, Cape Doctor wind shifts, and Robben Island restricted zones. A secondary support (kayak/paddler) is strongly recommended. For any record claim, an independent observer is required.
Recognition & validation
For the swim to be formally recognised beyond personal achievement:
·Independent observer present throughout
·GPS track from start to finish
·Feed log (time, content)
·Start & finish verification (witnessed land entry/exit)
·Ratified by Cape Long Distance Swimming Association or equivalent body
What doesn't count
✗"Swam roughly there and back" — not valid
✗Didn't physically land at the island — not valid
✗Out of water or stationary for more than ~10 minutes — becomes two swims
✗Touched the boat during feeds — not valid
✗Wetsuit — different category entirely
