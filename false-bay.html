False Bay

Miller's Point to Gordon's Bay — approximately 28 km across the open waters of False Bay. One of South Africa's most demanding and storied open water crossings.

Key Facts
Distance
~28 km
Miller's Point (Simon's Town) to Gordon's Bay. Route may vary based on conditions.
Water Temperature
14–18°C
Summer (Jan–Mar) sees the warmest water. The bay can be cold on its western side where Atlantic upwelling intrudes.
Swim Season
Jan – Mar
Southern hemisphere summer. Sea state is most settled in early morning; afternoon south-easterly wind typically builds.
Current
Variable
False Bay does not have strong tidal streams but localised currents near Cape Point can push swimmers off course.
Wildlife
Great whites
False Bay has one of the world's highest concentrations of great white sharks. Shark watch boats are required for any crossing attempt.
Difficulty
Extreme
Distance, cold, afternoon wind swell and shark risk combine to make this one of the hardest crossings in southern Africa.
What Makes It So Hard

Primary Hazards

  • Great white sharks — False Bay is a known great white aggregation site, particularly around Seal Island
  • The south-east wind ('the Cape Doctor') builds aggressively through the day, creating short steep chop
  • Distance across open water with no intermediate land — a 10+ hour commitment
  • Cold water intrusion from the Atlantic side can drop temperatures unexpectedly mid-crossing

Conditions Overview

  • Start windows are early morning (05:00–06:00) before the south-easter activates
  • Swell from the south-west enters the mouth of the bay and can create confused sea states
  • Water temperature varies across the bay — the Gordon's Bay end is warmer than the Simon's Town end
  • Visibility under water is generally good in settled conditions
About The Crossing

False Bay is a large bay on the south-western tip of South Africa, flanked by the Cape Peninsula on the west and the Hottentots Holland mountains on the east. The bay faces south-east, directly into the Southern Ocean, which drives the dominant swell and wind pattern.

A crossing of False Bay — typically from Miller's Point near Simon's Town to Gordon's Bay — covers approximately 28 km of open ocean. There is no protection from swell once away from the shore, and conditions deteriorate rapidly once the south-easterly wind builds, usually by mid-morning in summer.

The shark dimension is unique among the world's major crossings. False Bay is one of the few places where great white sharks congregate in numbers, drawn by the Cape fur seal colony at Seal Island in the centre of the bay. Any crossing attempt requires dedicated shark watch boats and an experienced team familiar with local wildlife protocols.

The crossing has been completed by a handful of elite South African open water swimmers. It is not a commercially organised event — every attempt is a private expedition requiring significant planning, permits and a well-prepared support team.

Preparation Guide

Training Benchmarks

  • Peak weekly volume: 40–55 km/week in the 12 weeks before your attempt
  • Longest training swim: 6–8 hours continuous in open water
  • Cold acclimatisation: regular swims in 14–16°C water — the Atlantic side of the Cape Peninsula is ideal
  • Ocean swimming experience: extensive experience with Cape conditions, swell and current
  • Night swimming optional but beneficial — start times before dawn

Cape Town's Atlantic Seaboard and False Bay both offer excellent training grounds. DUC (Down Under Club) at Clifton provides cold Atlantic open water experience. False Bay spots like St James, Fish Hoek and Gordon's Bay allow swimmers to familiarise themselves with the bay's characteristics.

Planning a False Bay crossing requires: permits from the relevant marine authority, a dedicated support team with shark watch boats, a meteorological briefing from a local specialist, and an emergency plan. The crossing is not undertaken lightly and is generally only attempted by swimmers with extensive local open water credentials.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is it safe to swim in False Bay given the sharks?
No crossing of False Bay can be considered without dedicated shark watch support. Multiple boats, shark spotters with experience in False Bay, and a clear abort protocol are required. Great white sightings mid-crossing are not uncommon, and any responsible attempt accounts for this with a robust safety plan.
What is the best time of year to attempt False Bay?
January to March offers the warmest water (16–18°C) and the most settled mornings. The window is narrow — the south-east wind activates reliably from late morning, so starts before 05:30 are typical to maximise calm water hours.
How does False Bay compare to Robben Island?
Robben Island (7.5 km) is the Cape Town crossing most swimmers target first. False Bay is nearly four times the distance, with the addition of shark risk and afternoon wind exposure. Completing Robben Island multiple times is considered a minimum prerequisite for planning False Bay.
Who has swum False Bay?
A small number of South African marathon swimmers have completed crossings of False Bay. It is not a regularly contested event — each crossing is a privately organised expedition. The crossing gained wider attention as South African open water swimming grew through the 2010s.
