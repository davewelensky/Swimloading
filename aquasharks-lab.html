Most of it goes straight down

Pressing water towards the bottom of the pool lifts your chest and drops your hips, so you end up dragging your own legs along behind you.

The rest goes sideways

If your hand sweeps across the middle of your body, it shoves you off your line. You wobble instead of travelling.

None of this is strength

You already make the force. It is pointing the wrong way. That is why this can change in a few sessions, while getting stronger takes years.

The best single swim measured in this lab got 62 out of 100 moving forward. More than double a typical club swimmer, from the same water and the same pair of arms.

How a session runs

01

Same warmup, same effort

Two 25m or two 50m efforts at about 75%, after a standard warmup. Identical every time, so session two is comparable to session one.

02

Sensors record every stroke

They sit in your hands. Nothing strapped to your body, nothing that changes how you swim.

03

We find the one thing

We read all four charts and pick the single dominant fault. Trying to fix five things at once is how you fix none of them.

04

Your report lands within 24 hours

What is working, the one thing to change, and the drill that changes it. Yours to keep, and to show your coach.

05

Come back and prove it held

Every retest is measured against your own baseline, not against anyone else.

We never coach against your coach. The report is yours, and we want you to share it with whoever trains you. It usually backs up what they have been telling you for months.
