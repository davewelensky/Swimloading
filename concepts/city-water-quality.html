Beach Alerts remains the authoritative City source. SwimLoading extends the reach of that information into the places where swimmers are already checking conditions and planning swims.

How this would work

The City's results would be shown where swimmers already plan their swim, attributed and linked back to Beach Alerts.

01
City publishes
Water-quality results, classifications and coastal alerts are published through Beach Alerts.
02
SwimLoading connects
City monitoring locations are matched to the appropriate SwimLoading swimming locations. Published results are then kept up to date automatically.
03
Swimmers see it
Shown on the spot page next to swimmer-logged water temperature, wind and swell.
  • City results and classifications are presented without SwimLoading recalculating or reclassifying them.
  • Every result carries its sample date, and older results are labelled as not recent rather than shown as current.
  • A single sample is always shown with the recent results and the long-term classification alongside it.
  • Active City coastal alerts are displayed prominently alongside the water-quality information and linked back to Beach Alerts.
Proof of concept prepared by SwimLoading for discussion with City of Cape Town Coastal Management. It does not represent a partnership with, or an endorsement by, the City. The Big Bay water-quality results and classifications are City of Cape Town data from Beach Alerts, captured on 25 Sep 2026 and not updated since. The failure, alert and no-recent-sample states are simulated for discussion and are not City results or alerts. Water-quality results describe samples taken on the dates shown; they do not mean the water is safe to swim in on the day. Water temperature is logged by SwimLoading swimmers; wind and swell come from the Open-Meteo forecast model.
