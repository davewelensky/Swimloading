How the Lagoon Tide Works
Langebaan Lagoon is a 17km enclosed lagoon connected to Saldanha Bay at its southern end, which in turn opens to the Atlantic. It has a semi-diurnal tide — two highs and two lows every ~24h 50min.

Flood tide (rising): Water flows into the lagoon from the south. Current moves northward through the lagoon — assisting the outbound leg from Preekstool toward Mykanos.

Ebb tide (falling): Water flows out of the lagoon to the south. Current moves southward — assisting the return leg from Mykanos back to Preekstool.

Ideal scenario: Start at low water (tide just beginning to flood). Swim north on flood for ~4–6 hours to Mykanos. Turn at or near high water. Swim south on ebb for ~4–6 hours back. Both legs assisted.

Tidal current speed: Typically 0.3–0.8 km/h in the lagoon. Stronger in the narrow channel near Langebaan town. This adds or costs 30–90 minutes over the full 24km.

Route Description
01
Start — Preekstool Beach
Enter at Preekstool Beach on the western shore of Langebaan Lagoon. Clear water entry under observer. The lagoon narrows near Langebaan town before opening into the main body northward.
02
Outbound — North through the lagoon
Swim approximately 12km north along the lagoon toward Mykanos resort. The route follows the western shoreline. Start on flood tide — the northward current will assist. Water is sheltered from ocean swell, but NW wind can create significant chop across the lagoon's width.
03
Turnaround — Mykanos
Touch at Mykanos on the northern lagoon. Aim to reach this point within ~10 minutes of high water — tide is turning, current neutral, conditions calmest. Quick feed and turn. The ~10-minute rest rule applies if competing for record status.
04
Return — South on the ebb
Swim 12km back south to Preekstool. The ebb tide now assists — current flows southward. Second leg typically feels faster as water temp stabilises and tidal assist builds. Wind may strengthen as the day progresses (NW in the afternoon).
05
Finish — Preekstool Beach
Clear water exit unassisted. Finish recorded from water entry to exit, land to land.
Key Requirements
01
Tide timing — the defining factor
Start at low water on a flood tide. This is non-negotiable for an optimally assisted double. Getting the tide wrong can add 1–2 hours and make the swim brutally difficult. Check the Tides tab for today's optimal window.
02
Wind — below 25 km/h preferred
The lagoon is sheltered from ocean swell but not from wind. The NW (northwesterly) is the prevailing wind here — it blows across the lagoon and creates short steep chop. Above 25 km/h makes navigation difficult and slows progress significantly. Flat mornings with afternoon NW are common — plan your start early.
03
Water temperature — 18°C minimum for skins
Langebaan Lagoon retains heat well — summer temperatures reach 20–24°C, far warmer than the adjacent Atlantic. Minimum 18°C recommended for a 10-hour skins swim. Check live community temps on the Now tab.
04
Support vessel
Kayak escort is standard. A motorised support vessel is not required but is advisable for safety coverage over the 24km route. Boat must respect the lagoon's no-wake zones and designated conservation areas.
05
West Coast National Park — permits
Langebaan Lagoon falls within the West Coast National Park. Check with WCNP for any swimming permits required, especially for organised events. The sanctuary zones at the northern end of the lagoon must be avoided — confirm the exact permitted route with park management before your swim.
Wildlife & Environment
Langebaan Lagoon is a globally significant wetland (RAMSAR site) — home to tens of thousands of migratory shorebirds, flamingos, and seabirds. The lagoon is also a haven for various marine species. Swimming conditions are generally safe — no significant shark risk in the enclosed lagoon. The water is crystal clear in summer, often with good underwater visibility.

Prediction Model — How It Works
Formula:
Total time = Leg 1 (12km ÷ pace ± tide assist) + Turnaround (10–15 min) + Leg 2 (12km ÷ pace ± tide assist)

Tide assist calibration:
· Both legs assisted (ideal start): +0.4 km/h per leg
· One leg assisted, one neutral: +0.2 km/h one leg
· One leg assisted, one opposed: neutral overall
· Both legs opposed: −0.3 km/h per leg

Pace adjustments:
· Skins in warm lagoon (20°C+): no adjustment
· Wetsuit: +0.3 km/h faster
· NW wind 20–30 km/h: −0.2 km/h
· NW wind 30+ km/h: −0.4 km/h

Model is based on typical lagoon swimming conditions. Individual fitness, feeding, and navigation add variability of ±45 minutes.
