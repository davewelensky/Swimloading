Community Safety Tips
Preventative Rabies Vaccination
2 injections one week apart, then a booster a year later. Eliminates the need for post-bite immunoglobulin if bitten by a seal. Consult your GP or Medicross.
Critical Safety Warnings
SEAL BITES / RABIES — MEDICAL EMERGENCY
Rabies is now considered endemic in Cape Fur Seals across the Western Cape coastline.
  • Any bite or scratch requires immediate hospital treatment — do not wait.
  • Wash wound with soap + water for 10–15 minutes.
  • Tell ER staff about potential rabies exposure — post-exposure treatment is time-critical.
  • Watch for seals acting lethargic, unusually friendly, or aggressive — red flags.
  • If beach closed after seal incident: siren + red flags will be activated.
HYPOTHERMIA — MEDICAL EMERGENCY
  • Remove wet clothing and shelter from wind.
  • Warm the core first (chest, neck, groin).
  • Handle gently — rough movement can trigger cardiac arrest.
  • Do not give alcohol or rub the skin.
Marine Life
SHARKS
  • Avoid dawn/dusk and murky water.
  • Exit water calmly if a shark is sighted.
  • Swim in groups where possible.
  • Follow all beach flags and shark alerts.
SEALS (INCL. RABID)
  • Do not approach or interact.
  • Exit immediately if a seal acts aggressively or unusually.
  • Erratic or overly friendly behaviour = DANGER.
BLUEBOTTLES / JELLYFISH
  • Rinse with seawater (never fresh water).
  • Exit water if stings are widespread.
  • Seek help if pain is severe or systemic.
SEWAGE / WATER POLLUTION
  • Do not swim if water is discoloured or smells foul.
  • Avoid swimming after heavy rainfall (stormwater runoff).
  • Check local municipality beach closures / advisories.
  • If exposed: shower immediately, avoid swallowing water.
  • Seek medical help if gastro, ear or skin infections develop.
Cold Water & Exposure
  • Cold + wind dramatically increases heat loss.
  • Shivering, slurred speech, confusion = DANGER SIGNS.
  • Get out early — do not "push through".
  • Always have a warm change and shelter ready.
